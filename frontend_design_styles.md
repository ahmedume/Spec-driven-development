Frontend Design Styles
A–Z reference guide
Each style is described in one concise line. Similar styles are kept separate where their visual approaches differ.
3D inflated → Puffy, balloon-like 3D forms with rounded volume, glossy materials, and playful proportions.
8-bit → Retro digital visuals built from coarse pixels and limited color palettes.
Anti-design → Intentionally rejects conventional visual rules through clashing layouts, awkward spacing, and raw elements.
Art Nouveau → Ornamental style featuring flowing organic lines, floral motifs, and decorative lettering.
Bauhaus → Functional modern design built from geometric forms, clean typography, and minimal ornament.
Bento grid → Modular layout that organizes content into neatly arranged, card-like blocks.
Bohemian → Eclectic, relaxed design mixing natural textures, earthy colors, and handcrafted details.
Brutalism → Raw, stark design that emphasizes structure, heavy typography, and unpolished elements.
Claymorphism → Soft, rounded 3D elements with matte surfaces and a playful, clay-like appearance.
Conceptual sketch → Hand-drawn, exploratory visuals that make a concept feel like an unfinished idea or blueprint.
Cybercore → Digital, internet-native aesthetic using futuristic interfaces, neon accents, and experimental graphics.
Cyberpunk → Dystopian futuristic style combining neon lights, dark palettes, and high-tech urban imagery.
De Stijl → Abstract modernist style using grids, rectangles, primary colors, and strong black lines.
Editorial design → Magazine-inspired layouts that use hierarchy, columns, imagery, and typography to guide reading.
Ethereal → Dreamlike, airy visuals using soft light, translucent layers, and delicate color palettes.
Glassmorphism → Frosted-glass panels with transparency, blur, and layered depth over colorful backgrounds.
Glitch → Digital distortion, visual noise, and broken-image effects used as deliberate design elements.
Grunge → Distressed, rough visuals featuring worn textures, irregular layouts, and rebellious typography.
Isometric → Three-dimensional-looking scenes drawn on angled axes without conventional perspective convergence.
Liquid chrome → Highly reflective, fluid metallic surfaces that create a futuristic, sculptural look.
Luxury typography → Elegant, high-contrast type and generous spacing that communicate refinement and exclusivity.
Maximalism → Dense, expressive design that layers bold colors, patterns, imagery, and competing details.
Memphis → Playful postmodern design with bright colors, geometric shapes, squiggles, and scattered patterns.
Mid-century modern → Clean, functional forms with organic curves, warm colors, and a restrained retro feel.
Minimalism → A pared-back approach that uses only essential elements, ample space, and clear hierarchy.
Neo-brutalism → Web design with bold colors, thick borders, hard shadows, and deliberately plain components.
Neomorphism → Soft UI surfaces that appear pressed or raised through subtle shadows matching the background.
Paper cutout → Layered shapes with paper-like edges and shadows that mimic handmade cut-paper collage.
Pixel art → Illustrations and interface graphics deliberately built from visible square pixels.
Pop art → Bold commercial imagery, strong outlines, and saturated colors inspired by mass culture and comics.
Psychedelic → Vivid, surreal visuals with warped forms, intense colors, and hallucinatory patterns.
Scrapbook → Collage-like layouts combining photos, notes, stickers, torn paper, and personal ephemera.
Skeuomorphism → Digital elements imitate real-world objects, materials, and controls to feel familiar.
Steampunk → Victorian-inspired machinery, brass, gears, and industrial details imagined through retro-futurism.
Surrealism → Unexpected dreamlike combinations and impossible imagery that challenge ordinary reality.
Swiss design → Orderly grid-based layouts with strong alignment, sans-serif type, and clear visual hierarchy.
Synthwave → Retro-futuristic visuals inspired by 1980s neon sunsets, grids, and glowing gradients.
Typography → Design that makes letterforms, type scale, spacing, and text arrangement the primary visual focus.
Ukiyo-e → Traditional Japanese woodblock-inspired imagery with flat colors, flowing contours, and stylized scenes.
Vaporwave → Nostalgic digital collage using pastel neon, retro technology, classical imagery, and surreal internet motifs.
Victorian → Ornate, historically inspired design with intricate borders, decorative type, and rich detail.
Wabi-sabi → A Japanese-inspired aesthetic that values imperfection, natural materials, simplicity, and age.
Y2K → Late-1990s and early-2000s futurism with chrome, glossy gradients, bubbly forms, and digital nostalgia.
YSK aesthetic → An informal, non-standardized label; its intended visual traits depend on the community or reference using it.
